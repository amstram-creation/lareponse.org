Why digital work becomes difficult

Most difficulty enters before development begins.

A solution is named too early

“We need a portal”, “we need AI” or “we need a redesign” may describe an assumed answer rather than the work that must change.

The inherited field is ignored

Existing systems, habits, data, contracts and informal workarounds shape what is possible. New work never begins on a blank page.

Scope is expressed as objects

Pages, screens and features are listed without specifying who needs them, what behaviour must change, or how success will be verified.

Responsibility remains implicit

The client, provider and third parties may each assume that someone else owns decisions, content, access, data quality, testing or adoption.

Delivery and learning are confused

A genuine defect, a changed preference, a new requirement and a newly discovered constraint are treated as though they were the same thing.

Complexity survives its purpose

Features, procedures and integrations accumulate even after the work that justified them has moved elsewhere.

The result is often not a visible failure. It is drift: more activity, more structure and more cost, with less correspondence between the system and the work it is meant to support.

THE LARÉPONSE RESPONSE

Begin with what is happening. Identify the smallest complete intervention that can make a useful difference. Let actual use reveal what should be preserved, corrected, extended, simplified or released.

CAPABILITY & PARTNERSHIP

How we think about the work

Six principles keep delivery connected to reality.

1. Contact before category

We examine the actual users, interactions, burdens, constraints and consequences before accepting inherited labels as the definition of the problem.

2. An exact first move before a total solution

The first intervention does not need to contain the final system. It must change the present situation clearly enough to produce a useful response.

3. Use before abstraction

We learn from real behaviour. Recurrence becomes evidence; evidence reveals pattern; pattern justifies standardisation.

4. Responsibility before structure

Roles, governance and process should carry real responsibilities. Structure without responsibility becomes overhead.

5. Continuation by evidence, not inertia

A system should continue because it remains useful and generative—not merely because it already exists.

6. Build within the inherited field

We preserve valuable assets, work with genuine constraints and treat old systems as a source of both limitation and reusable material.

THE WORKING SEQUENCE

Need → exact intervention → use → evidence → pattern → capability → durable operation

This sequence does not prevent ambition. It disciplines ambition. Larger possibilities remain visible, but they are approached through decisions that can be understood, tested and owned.

Clarify the work

A clear brief is not a longer brief. It is a testable agreement.

We turn ambiguous requests into a shared account of what is happening, what must become possible, and what evidence will show that the intervention has worked.

Situation and pressure

What has changed? Where is friction visible? Why does the issue matter now?

People and relationships

Who acts, decides, provides, receives, approves, supports or bears the consequence?

Existing field

Which systems, data, contracts, habits, workarounds and assets already shape the work?

Required behaviour

What must a user or system be able to do, under which conditions, with which permissions and outcomes?

Acceptance and evidence

How will we distinguish a working result from an impression that the work is “finished”?

Dependencies and uncertainty

What must be supplied, decided, accessed or tested before confidence can increase?

TYPICAL DEFINITION OUTPUTS

  • Situation brief and problem framing
  • Stakeholder, user and workflow map
  • Inventory of relevant systems, data and dependencies
  • Prioritised feature or outcome definition
  • Acceptance criteria and evidence plan
  • Assumption, risk and decision log
  • Recommended first intervention and optional future horizons
EXAMPLE

“We need a client portal” becomes: which clients, which tasks, which information, which permissions, which service levels, which systems of record, and which observable outcome.

Stabilise

Hand over

TYPICAL STABILISATION WORK

  • Functional and acceptance testing
  • Defect triage and correction
  • Performance, reliability and accessibility checks appropriate to scope
  • Release and deployment support
  • Monitoring and operational documentation
  • Administrator or team handover
  • Known-issue, dependency and follow-up register
MAINTENANCE AND EVOLUTION ARE DIFFERENT

Maintenance preserves agreed behaviour. Evolution changes, expands or reinterprets that behaviour. Keeping the distinction visible protects both quality and commercial clarity.

Not every observed request should become a feature. Repetition may reveal a pattern worth supporting; it may also reveal training, process, ownership or policy issues that software should not conceal.

CAPABILITY & PARTNERSHIP

Commercial engagement models

The commercial form should match the uncertainty and continuity of the work.

Definition engagement

For an important need that is not yet precise enough to price or build responsibly. Produces a situation brief, first intervention and decision-ready scope.

Defined delivery

For a feature set or outcome that can be agreed in advance. Scope, assumptions, acceptance and commercial terms are documented before construction.

Capability partnership

For continuing product or operational evolution. Work is prioritised in cycles and delivered through an agreed capacity, retainer or development-credit model.

WHERE DEVELOPMENT CREDITS ARE USED

  • Requests are converted into a defined feature, task or investigation before credits are consumed.
  • Priority and estimated credit use are made visible before work begins.
  • Usage, remaining balance and status remain transparent.
  • Verified defects are treated according to the agreed acceptance and warranty terms, not silently converted into new work.
  • Urgent, exceptional or out-of-hours work is agreed separately where it requires different capacity or risk.

Exact pricing, credit values, validity, warranty, service levels and payment terms belong in the proposal or agreement for the specific engagement.

COMMERCIAL PRINCIPLE

The client should understand what is being purchased, which uncertainty remains, what evidence will be produced and what decision follows.

Defects, changes and new learning

The same symptom can imply different responsibilities. Classification should follow the agreed definition and evidence.

TYPEMEANINGTREATMENT
DefectDelivered behaviour does not meet an agreed acceptance criterion under the agreed conditions.Triage, reproduce and correct under the applicable delivery or warranty terms.
ChangeThe desired behaviour differs from, expands or replaces the agreed definition.Define, estimate, prioritise and commission as new work.
InvestigationThe cause, ownership or appropriate solution is not yet known.Time-box diagnosis, gather evidence, then classify the resulting work.
External issueBehaviour is caused by a third-party platform, vendor, data source, policy or environment outside direct control.Document impact, mitigate where possible and coordinate escalation or alternatives.
Operational or adoption issueThe system behaves as agreed, but process, content, training, ownership or usage prevents the intended outcome.Address the relevant operating condition rather than treating every issue as a software feature.

This distinction is not used to avoid responsibility. It is used to place responsibility accurately, protect the quality baseline and make new decisions explicit.

What we could do together

“Could” describes an opportunity hypothesis—not work already sold.

The most valuable future capability is often not visible at the beginning. It becomes legible when the first intervention enters real use and patterns begin to repeat.

SIGNALS THAT MAY JUSTIFY A LARGER CAPABILITY

  • The same request, exception or workaround recurs across users or teams.
  • A responsibility exists in practice but has no dependable system, role or record around it.
  • Customers repeatedly ask for access, visibility or self-service that the current model cannot provide.
  • Decisions depend on information that exists but is fragmented, late or difficult to trust.
  • An existing system preserves an outdated procedure while the actual work has moved elsewhere.
  • A successful local solution is being copied informally and now requires a shared standard.
  • The cost of coordination has become greater than the work being coordinated.

Now

The first defined intervention: committed, scoped and verifiable.

Next

An adjacent capability considered only if agreed evidence appears.

Later

A strategic opportunity territory that informs architecture and choices without becoming premature scope.

OUR DISCIPLINE

We keep later possibilities visible so the first move does not create a dead end. We do not present those possibilities as inevitable outcomes or commitments.
